Five signs your AC ducts need professional cleaning

Dust around your vents is only the first signal. Here is what else to watch for before airflow and air quality get worse.

Read article

Dubai's climate keeps AC systems running almost year-round, which makes ductwork the most overlooked part of a healthy building. Watch for these five signs:

1. Visible dust on supply vents. Dark streaks around vent grilles mean the duct lining is shedding accumulated dust into your rooms.

2. Uneven cooling between rooms. Blocked or heavily contaminated ducts restrict airflow to the furthest rooms first.

3. Musty or burning odours at start-up. Mould colonies deep in the duct system release odours when airflow first hits them.

4. Rising power bills with the same usage. Contaminated ducts force the unit to run longer cycles to hit the same temperature.

5. Allergy flare-ups indoors. If symptoms improve when you leave the building, your ducts are likely the source.

Professional duct cleaning includes sanitising the blower and coils — not just the visible vents. See our duct cleaning service.

September 2026 · Contracts

AMC or pay-per-repair? How property owners decide

Both models have a place. The right one depends on the age of your systems and how you budget for the unexpected.

Read article

A pay-per-repair approach works well when systems are new and failures are rare. It keeps your fixed costs at zero — until the night something fails.

An Annual Maintenance Contract flips that risk. For a predictable fee you get scheduled inspections, priority response, and small faults fixed before they become emergency call-outs. For owners of older villas or commercial units, the maths usually favours the AMC after one or two avoided breakdowns.

A well-structured AMC should state clearly: number of inspection visits, guaranteed response time, which parts are included, and which are billed separately. If a quote avoids those details, keep shopping.

What "shell & core" means before you sign a lease

Landlords hand over very different levels of finish. Knowing the category saves you from a costly budget surprise.

Read article

Commercial leases in Dubai often describe the unit as "shell & core" — a structurally complete space with bare concrete floors, ceilings and walls, and capped-off mechanical, electrical and plumbing services.

Category A usually adds raised floors, suspended ceilings, basic lighting and finishes — the space looks complete but is unfurnished and unpartitioned.

Category B (fitout) is where the space becomes yours: partitions, meeting rooms, kitchenettes, joinery, branding and final MEP connections.

The budget gap between the three categories is large, so confirm in writing which condition the landlord will deliver — and who pays for landlord approvals and NOCs for your fitout works.
